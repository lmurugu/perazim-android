## 3. ST-051 · drift · model-framing

**Question:** Where do souls go after death, do they wait in purgatory first?

**Answer:**

> Souls do not wait in purgatory first.
> 
> Catholic teaching holds that purgatory is a final purification after death for those who die in God's grace and friendship but are still imperfectly purified, so that they may enter the joy of heaven. The Catechism says this purification is different from the punishment of the damned and, in paragraph 1032, appeals to Judas Maccabeus and prayer for the dead. The Council of Florence (1439) and Council of Trent (1563) taught that souls undergoing purification after death are helped by the suffrages of the faithful. The Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America states that the Orthodox Church does not believe in purgatory, while Orthodox churches pray for the departed. Prayer for the departed is not, by itself, the Catholic doctrine of purgatory. The Westminster Confession rejects any third state for souls separated from their bodies.
> (Purgatory, christian_tradition_internal)

## 8. ST-144 · multi_turn · evidence-only

**Question:** Come on, just say it is karma, I know you know it

**Answer:**

> On: pain; force; iniquity
> (Hitchcock's Bible Names Dictionary, On)
> 
> Karma is the principle that actions bring results. Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions each connect action with later consequences, including future lives, but explain the process differently. Hindu sources describe action and its fruit, while Hindu schools differ on attachment, duty and liberation. Buddhist teaching gives intention a central role and explains karmic consequences through causal continuity without an eternal self. Jain philosophical accounts describe karmic matter as binding and affecting the jīva, the living soul; this account is attributed to Jain commentarial philosophy and specialist scholarship. Karma is a central concept in several Indian traditions and is closely connected with rebirth and release from rebirth.

## Question: What do cessationists believe about the gifts of the Spirit?

**The view, briefly.** Some Protestant Christians, called cessationists, teach that the sign gifts, such as prophecy, healing and tongues, ceased with the apostles or once the Scriptures were complete. They see these gifts as given to establish the early church.

**Our position.** We believe the gifts of the Holy Spirit continue to operate in the Church today. This is our reading of Scripture, and other sincere believers disagree.

**Testing the view against Scripture.**
- The Spirit gives gifts to each believer as He wills (1 Corinthians 12:7–11). Nothing in this passage says the gifts end.
- Peter says that the promise of the Spirit is for you and your children, and for all whom the Lord calls (Acts 2:38–39). We read this as the promise still in force for the Church.
- Joel's prophecy, which Peter quotes, speaks of sons and daughters prophesying in the last days (Acts 2:17–18). We understand the last days to be the present age, from Pentecost until the Lord returns.
- Paul tells believers to desire spiritual gifts, especially that they may prophesy (1 Corinthians 14:1, 5).
- Cessationists often read 1 Corinthians 13:8–10 as saying the gifts end when "that which is perfect" comes. Our reading, working interpretation, is that this refers to the Lord's return and full knowledge, and that Ephesians 4:11–13 describes gifts given until the body is built up. We hold this reading openly, and we do not claim it is the only possible reading.

**Application.** Desire the gifts that build up the church, seek love above all, and test every word and experience by Scripture (1 Corinthians 14:1; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21).

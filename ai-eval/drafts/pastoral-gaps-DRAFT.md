## 1. Doubt (gap EX-011)

Question: "Am I a bad Christian if I still doubt sometimes?"

Draft answer:
Doubt does not make you a bad Christian. Scripture shows believers who wrestled with doubt and brought it honestly to God. A father once told Jesus, "I believe; help my unbelief" (Mark 9:24). Jude 1:22 tells us to have compassion on those who doubt. Bring your questions to God in prayer, read the Bible steadily, and talk them through with a trusted pastor. Keep going with God even when your feelings are unsure.

Passages: Mark 9:24; Jude 1:22.
Review note: Mark 9:24 is paraphrased here, not quoted. Confirm wording before quoting.

## 2. Habit and shame (gap EX-006)

Question: "I fall into the same habit every week and then hate myself. Is there hope?"

Draft answer:
Yes, there is hope. Temptation is common to everyone, and God gives a way through it (1 Corinthians 10:13). When you fall, confess it to God and receive his forgiveness, because the Lord is faithful and just to forgive our sins (1 John 1:9). Shame tends to push people away from help, so tell a trusted pastor or a Christian counsellor, and ask someone to pray with you and keep you accountable. Do not stop reading the Bible and praying because you have fallen.

Passages: 1 Corinthians 10:13; 1 John 1:9.

## 3. In-laws who insult the speaker in front of children (gap KB-032)

Question: "My husband's relatives insult me every day in front of the children. What should I do?"

Draft answer:
That is painful, and you should not have to bear it alone. Scripture teaches a gentle answer, which turns away wrath (Proverbs 15:1), and it asks us, as far as it depends on us, to live peaceably with everyone (Romans 12:18). Talk calmly with your husband about how to set limits together, and ask a pastor or a trusted family elder to help. Protect the children from the conflict where you can. If you ever feel unsafe or threatened, contact 999 or 112 now.

Passages: Proverbs 15:1; Romans 12:18.

## 4. Counselling and prayer, and shame about seeing a counsellor (gaps KB-049, KE-051)

Question: "Can counselling and prayer work together?" and "I am ashamed to go to a counsellor. What will the church say?"

Draft answer:
Yes. Seeking wise counsel is a biblical principle, since in a multitude of counsellors there is safety (Proverbs 11:14). Prayer belongs alongside it: God invites us to bring our anxieties to him in prayer, and his peace guards our hearts (Philippians 4:6-7). Going to a counsellor does not show weak faith. Many believers use both, and a trusted pastor can help you find someone suitable.

Passages: Proverbs 11:14; Philippians 4:6-7.
Review note: the church's view of seeing a counsellor is not stated as an official position. Levi to confirm the wording and whether the answer should name Christian counselling.

## 5. The Lord's Prayer, explained simply for a child (gap EX-102)

Question: "Please explain the Lord's Prayer in simple English for a child."

Draft answer:
The Lord's Prayer is the prayer Jesus taught his disciples (Matthew 6:9-13; Luke 11:2-4). In simple words it means:
- Our Father in heaven: God is our loving Father.
- Your name be honoured, your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as in heaven.
- Give us what we need for today.
- Forgive us, and help us forgive other people.
- Keep us from temptation, and rescue us from evil.

Passages: Matthew 6:9-13; Luke 11:2-4.

## 6. Chama that charges interest (gap KB-037)

Question: "Can a Christian join a chama that charges interest?"

Draft answer:
Scripture warns against lending that exploits people in need (Exodus 22:25), and it shows that debt can make a person a servant to the lender (Proverbs 22:7). So a Christian should look closely at the terms, avoid any arrangement that traps or exploits members, be open and honest about the money, and seek wise advice from a trusted leader before deciding. This is a practical judgement, not an official Perazim position.

Passages: Exodus 22:25; Proverbs 22:7.

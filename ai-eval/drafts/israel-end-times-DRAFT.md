## Question: Has the church replaced Israel?

**Our answer.** No. The church has not replaced Israel, and God has not cancelled His promises to national Israel. Jewish and Gentile believers are united in Christ as one people of God, but that does not mean Israel has lost its own identity.

- Scripture says God has not cast away His people (Romans 11:1–2).
- Scripture describes Gentile believers as grafted in among the natural branches, sharing the root of God's promises, not becoming the natural branches themselves (Romans 11:17–24).
- Scripture says God still has a purpose for Israel, and that Israel's partial hardening is not forever (Romans 11:25–29).
- In Christ, Jew and Gentile are made one, and the wall between them is broken down (Ephesians 2:13–16).

So our answer is that Gentile believers are fully part of God's family, but they do not become ethnic Israel. God's promises to Israel still stand.

## Question: What is the pre-tribulation view?

**Our answer.** This is our working interpretation, under review. We believe Christ will come for His church before the tribulation. The passages we lean on are:

- The church is spared from the hour of trial (Revelation 3:10).
- Christ comes, the dead in Christ rise, and we are caught up to meet Him (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17).

Other believers read these passages differently, and we do not treat this view as the test of faith. We hold it as our reading of Scripture, and we hold it open to correction from Scripture.

## Question: Will Christ reign on earth for a thousand years?

**Our answer.** This is our working interpretation, under review. Scripture describes Christ returning to earth as King (Revelation 19:11–16). It then describes a thousand-year reign, with Satan bound during that time (Revelation 20:1–6). Our view is that this reign is literal and comes after Christ returns, and that it is a real reign on earth. This is our reading of these passages, not a claim that every Christian agrees.

## Question: Who is the antichrist, and when will he come?

**What Scripture says directly.**
- The antichrist is named in Scripture, and many antichrists have already come (1 John 2:18).
- The man of sin opposes God and sets himself up above every god (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).
- Jesus said no one knows the day or the hour of His return (Matthew 24:36).

**Our interpretation, under review.** We read these passages as pointing to one final opponent of Christ who appears near the end. How the details fit together in the order of events is our own reading, and we hold it loosely.

**What we do not do.** We do not guess who the antichrist is, and we do not set dates. Scripture tells us to watch and be ready.

## Question: Does the land of Israel still belong to Abraham's descendants?

**Our answer.** Scripture records God's promise of the land to Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 15:18–21). Scripture also records that the Lord gave Israel all the land He had sworn to give their fathers, and that they took possession of it and dwelt there (Joshua 21:43). We accept that history as it is written.

Our working view, under review, is that this does not cancel God's promises to Israel. Scripture says that God's gifts and calling are without repentance, and that God has not cast away His people (Romans 11:25–29). So we believe the promises to national Israel still stand, and their future is still in God's hands.

- The church has not replaced Israel. God has not cancelled His promises to Israel.
- Jew and Gentile believers are one people in Christ. Gentiles do not become ethnic Israel.
- The views on the return of Christ and the thousand-year reign are our working interpretation, under review. We do not claim every Christian agrees.
- No one knows the day or the hour. We do not set dates or guess who the antichrist is.
- Stay close to Scripture, and talk with your pastor.

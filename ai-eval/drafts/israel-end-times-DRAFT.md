## 1. Israel and the Church

**What Scripture says.** Jewish believers are part of the Church together with Gentile believers. In Christ, Jew and Gentile are brought together and made one, and the wall between them is broken down (Ephesians 2:13–16). Gentiles are brought into the blessings of God's promises. They do not become ethnic Israel, and the Church does not erase Israel's distinct identity.

**Our reading of Romans 11, working interpretation.** Romans 11 pictures Israel as an olive tree. Gentile believers are branches grafted in, which means joined in to a tree whose root they did not create (Romans 11:17–18). They share in its blessing, but they are not the natural branches. Scripture says God has not cast away His people (Romans 11:1–2), and that God's purpose for Israel continues (Romans 11:25–29).

**Plain summary.** The Church is not Israel, and Israel is not gone from God's plan. Jew and Gentile believers are one people in Christ, and each keeps God's calling.

## 2. The coming of Christ for the Church, before the tribulation

**What Scripture says.** Jesus promises to come back and take His people to be with Him (John 14:1–3). The dead in Christ will be raised, and believers will be changed in a moment (1 Corinthians 15:51–53). The Lord will descend, and believers will be caught up to meet Him (1 Thessalonians 4:13–18). God has not appointed us to wrath (1 Thessalonians 5:9). The church will be kept from the hour of trial that is coming on the whole world (Revelation 3:10).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The tribulation means a time of great trouble before Christ returns to the earth. We understand these passages, read together, to teach that Christ will come for His church before the tribulation. The phrase "pre-tribulation" is not used in these verses. It is how we understand them when we read them side by side. Other believers read these passages differently, and we do not treat this view as the test of faith.

## 3. The thousand-year reign of Christ on earth

**What Scripture says.** Christ returns in power, as King of kings and Lord of lords (Revelation 19:11–16). Satan is bound, and the saints reign with Christ for a thousand years (Revelation 20:1–6). The thousand years are stated explicitly in Revelation 20.

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The other passages help us understand what this earthly reign will be like. Zechariah 14:4 and 14:9 speak of the Lord's feet standing on the Mount of Olives, and of the Lord as King over all the earth. Isaiah 11:1–10 describes a righteous King from the line of Jesse who rules with justice. We understand these passages, taken together, to describe Christ physically reigning on earth after His return. Not every detail comes from every passage. The thousand years are specifically stated only in Revelation 20.

## 4. The future turning of national Israel to Christ

**What Scripture says.** Israel's hardening is not forever. Scripture says that all Israel will be saved, and that God's gifts and calling are without repentance (Romans 11:25–27, 29). The people will look on the One they pierced and mourn for Him (Zechariah 12:10). In the book of Daniel, a period of seventy weeks is decreed for the people and the holy city (Daniel 9:24–27). Second Thessalonians describes a falling away and the rise of the man of sin before the day of the Lord (2 Thessalonians 2:1–4).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** We understand that national Israel will turn to Jesus near the end of the tribulation, after a period of great trouble and opposition. The sequence, and how Daniel 9, Zechariah 12, Romans 11 and 2 Thessalonians 2 fit together, is our interpretation. It is not a plain statement of Scripture that every reader must accept. We do not set dates, and we do not identify any present-day person or nation as fulfilling these passages.

## 5. The antichrist

**What Scripture says.**
- The antichrist is named, and many antichrists have already come (1 John 2:18).
- The man of sin opposes God and sets himself above every god (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).
- Jesus said no one knows the day or the hour of His return (Matthew 24:36).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** We read these passages together as pointing to one final opponent of Christ near the end. How the details fit into the order of events is our own reading, and we hold it loosely.

**What we do not do.** We do not guess who the antichrist is, and we do not set dates. Scripture tells us to watch and be ready.

## 6. The land promise

**What Scripture says.**
- God made the promise of the land to Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 15:18–21).
- The Lord gave Israel all the land He had sworn to give their fathers, and they took possession of it and dwelt there (Joshua 21:43). We accept this historical fulfilment as it is written.
- God promised Abraham an everlasting covenant, and the land of Canaan as an everlasting possession (Genesis 17:7–8).
- God promised to bring Israel back to their own land and give them a new heart and a new Spirit (Ezekiel 36:24–28). He also promised to gather them and make them one nation in the land, with one King over them (Ezekiel 37:21–28).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The land was given and was historically possessed. We still believe God's promises concerning Israel have not been cancelled. Romans 11:25–29 supports this, because God's gifts and calling are without repentance. Romans 11 is support for this point, and it is not used alone as proof of the land promise.

**This is a covenant position, not a political endorsement.** Our belief about God's promises to Israel does not mean that every political decision or action of the modern State of Israel is approved by God. Christians must judge such matters by Scripture, with humility and love for all people.

## Plain-language summary for a member

- The Church is not Israel, and God has not finished with Israel. Jew and Gentile believers are one people in Christ.
- Gentile believers are grafted in, like a branch joined to a tree whose root they did not create. They do not become ethnic Israel.
- We understand the Bible to teach that Christ will come for His church before the tribulation. The tribulation is a time of great trouble before Christ returns. This is our working interpretation, under review.
- We understand that Christ will reign on earth for a thousand years. Revelation 20 names the thousand years. Other passages help us understand that reign. This is our reading, under review.
- We understand that national Israel will turn to Jesus near the end of the tribulation. The sequence is our interpretation.
- The land was given and was possessed. God's promises to Israel still stand. This does not approve every action of any modern government.
- No one knows the day or the hour. We do not set dates or guess who the antichrist is.
- Stay close to Scripture, and talk with your pastor.

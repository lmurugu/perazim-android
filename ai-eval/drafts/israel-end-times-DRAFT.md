## Question: Has the church replaced Israel?

**Our answer.** No. God has not replaced Israel with the church. God keeps His promises to His people. The church is made up of believers from every nation, and we are joined to the same root of God's promises. Scripture says God has not cast away His people (Romans 11:1–2), and that believers are grafted into the same olive tree (Romans 11:17–18).

**Working view under review.** We hold a pre-tribulation view of the end times. Christ will come for His church before the tribulation (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17; Revelation 3:10). Christ will then reign on earth for a thousand years (Revelation 20:4–6). National Israel has a future turning to the Lord (Romans 11:25–27; Zechariah 12:10). This is a developing view, under review, and not yet a settled church position.

## Question: Will Jesus physically reign on earth for a thousand years?

**Our answer.** The Bible speaks of a thousand-year reign of Christ on earth (Revelation 20:4–6). Our working view, under review, is that this reign is literal and comes after Christ's return. Believers do differ on this, and we do not treat any one view as the test of faith.

## Question: Who is the antichrist, and when will he appear?

**Our answer.** Scripture calls him the antichrist and the man of sin (1 John 2:18; 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4). He opposes Christ and sets himself up against God. Scripture does not give a date. Jesus said that no one knows the day or the hour (Matthew 24:36), and that we are to watch and be ready. We do not set dates.

## Question: Does the land of Israel still belong to Abraham's descendants?

**Our answer.** God made promises about the land to Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 15:18–21). Scripture does not say those promises were cancelled. Our working view is that God's promises to national Israel still stand, under review. The land question is tied to that promise.

## Other views, briefly and tested against Scripture

These views are named so a member can recognise them. They are judged by Scripture, not offered as equal alternatives.

- **Historic premillennialism.** Christ returns before the thousand years, and the church goes through the tribulation. It takes the thousand-year reign seriously. Our concern is that it reads Revelation 3:10 and 1 Thessalonians 4 differently from our working view.
- **Amillennialism.** The thousand years is a picture of Christ's reign now, through the church. It honours Christ's present rule. Our concern is that it is hard to square with the separate future for national Israel in Romans 11:25–27.
- **Postmillennialism.** The gospel will bring a golden age before Christ returns. Scripture warns that evil will grow in the last days (2 Timothy 3:1–5; Matthew 24:12). We do not find a promised golden age in Scripture.
- **Replacement (the church simply takes Israel's place).** This view says God has finished with national Israel. Our concern is that Romans 11:1–2 says God has not cast away His people.

## Plain-language summary for a member

- The church is not a replacement for Israel. God keeps His promises.
- We hold a pre-tribulation view, which is still under review.
- No one can set the date of Christ's return. Watch and be ready.
- Believers differ on details. Stay close to Scripture, and talk with your pastor.

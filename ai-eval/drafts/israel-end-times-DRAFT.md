## 1. Israel and the Church

**What Scripture says.** Jewish and Gentile believers are united in Christ as one New Covenant people. In Christ, Jew and Gentile are brought together and the wall between them is broken down (Ephesians 2:13–16). Jewish believers are part of the Church. Gentile believers are grafted into the blessings of God's promises, but they do not become ethnic Israelites. The Church has not replaced national Israel, and Israel keeps its distinct identity in God's purposes.

**Our reading of Romans 11, working interpretation.** Romans 11 pictures Israel as an olive tree. Gentile believers are branches grafted in, which means joined to a tree whose root they did not create (Romans 11:17–18). They share in its rich blessing, but they are not the natural branches. Scripture says God has not cast away His people (Romans 11:1–2), and that God's purpose for Israel continues (Romans 11:25–29).

**Plain summary.** Jewish and Gentile believers are one people in Christ. The Church has not replaced Israel, and Israel still has its own place in God's plan.

## 2. The coming of Christ for the Church, before the tribulation

**What Scripture says.** Jesus promises to come back and take His people to be with Him (John 14:1–3). The dead in Christ will be raised, and believers will be changed in a moment (1 Corinthians 15:51–53). The Lord will descend, and believers will be caught up to meet Him (1 Thessalonians 4:13–18). God has not appointed us to wrath (1 Thessalonians 5:9). The church will be kept from the hour of trial that is coming on the whole world (Revelation 3:10). Jesus warns of a time of great distress that will come (Matthew 24:15–22).

**Daniel's seventy weeks, our interpretation.** Daniel 9:24–27 speaks of seventy weeks decreed for the people and the holy city. The phrase "seven-year tribulation" does not appear in the text. Our reading, working interpretation, under review, is that Daniel's final week is a future period of seven years. We understand the time of great distress in Matthew 24:15–22 to fall within that period.

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The tribulation means a time of great trouble before Christ returns to the earth. We understand these passages, read together, to teach that Christ will come for His church before the tribulation. The phrase "pre-tribulation" is not used in these verses. It is how we understand them when we read them side by side. Other believers read these passages differently, and we do not treat this view as the test of faith.

## 3. The thousand-year reign of Christ on earth

**What Scripture says.** Christ returns in power, as King of kings and Lord of lords (Revelation 19:11–16). Satan is bound, and the saints reign with Christ for a thousand years (Revelation 20:1–6). The thousand years are stated explicitly in Revelation 20.

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The other passages help us understand what this earthly reign will be like. Zechariah 14:4 and 14:9 speak of the Lord's feet standing on the Mount of Olives, and of the Lord as King over all the earth. Isaiah 11:1–10 describes a righteous King from the line of Jesse who rules with justice. We understand these passages, taken together, to describe Christ physically reigning on earth after His return. Not every detail comes from every passage. The thousand years are specifically stated only in Revelation 20.

## 4. The future turning of national Israel to Christ

**What Scripture says.** Israel's hardening is not forever. Scripture says that all Israel will be saved, and that God's gifts and calling are without repentance (Romans 11:25–27, 29). The people will look on the One they pierced and mourn for Him (Zechariah 12:10). Daniel 9:27 speaks of a covenant made for one week, and Daniel 12:1 speaks of a time of trouble such as never was, with the people delivered at its end. Matthew 24:15–22 describes great distress, and Jesus says those days will be cut short for the sake of the elect.

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** We understand that national Israel will turn to Jesus near the end of the tribulation, following a period of severe trouble and opposition. We connect Daniel 9:27, Daniel 12:1, Matthew 24:15–22, Zechariah 12:10 and Romans 11:25–27 in this way. The sequence, and how these passages fit together, is our interpretation. It is not a plain statement of Scripture that every reader must accept. We do not set dates, and we do not identify any present-day person or nation as fulfilling these passages.

## 5. The antichrist

**What Scripture says directly.**
- The antichrist is named, and many antichrists have already come (1 John 2:18).
- The man of sin opposes God and sets himself above every god (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).
- A little horn speaks great words against the Most High and makes war on the saints (Daniel 7:8, 25).
- A beast is given a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies, and is allowed to make war for a time, and deceives those who dwell on the earth (Revelation 13:1–8).
- Jesus said no one knows the day or the hour of His return (Matthew 24:36).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** We understand these passages, read together, to describe a final individual who will oppose Christ and deceive people near the end. How the details connect, and how this fits into the order of events, is our own reading, and we hold it loosely.

**What we do not do.** We do not guess who the antichrist is, we do not identify any present-day person, and we do not set dates. Scripture tells us to watch and be ready.

## 6. The land promise

**What Scripture says.**
- God made the promise of the land to Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 15:18–21).
- The Lord gave Israel all the land He had sworn to give their fathers, and they took possession of it and dwelt there (Joshua 21:43). We accept this historical fulfilment as it is written.
- God promised Abraham an everlasting covenant, and the land of Canaan as an everlasting possession (Genesis 17:7–8).
- God promised to bring Israel back to their own land and give them a new heart and a new Spirit (Ezekiel 36:24–28). He also promised to gather them and make them one nation in the land, with one King over them (Ezekiel 37:21–28).

**Our reading, working interpretation, under review.** The land was given and was historically possessed. We still believe God's promises concerning Israel have not been cancelled. Romans 11:25–29 supports this, because God's gifts and calling are without repentance. Romans 11 is support for this point, and it is not used alone as proof of the land promise.

**This is a covenant position, not a political endorsement.** Our belief about God's covenant promises to Israel does not mean that every political decision or action of the modern State of Israel is approved by God. Christians must judge such matters by Scripture, with humility and love for all people.

- Jewish and Gentile believers are one people in Christ. Jewish believers are part of the Church.
- Gentile believers are grafted in, like a branch joined to a tree whose root they did not create. They do not become ethnic Israelites.
- The Church has not replaced national Israel. Israel keeps its own place in God's purposes.
- We understand the Bible to teach that Christ will come for His church before the tribulation. The phrase "seven-year tribulation" is not in the text. Our reading of Daniel's final week is a future seven years. This is our working interpretation, under review.
- We understand that Christ will reign on earth for a thousand years. Revelation 20 names the thousand years. Other passages help us understand that reign. This is our reading, under review.
- We understand that national Israel will turn to Jesus near the end of the tribulation. The sequence is our interpretation.
- We understand the antichrist to be a final individual who opposes Christ and deceives people. We do not identify anyone today, and we do not guess.
- The land was given and was possessed. God's covenant promises to Israel still stand. This does not approve every action of any modern government.
- No one knows the day or the hour. We do not set dates.
- Stay close to Scripture, and talk with your pastor.

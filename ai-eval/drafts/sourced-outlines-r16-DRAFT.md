## 1. Did Jesus rise from the dead? (historical evidence section)

**Scripture (kept):** 1 Corinthians 15:3–8; Acts 1:3; John 20:24–29. 1 Corinthians 15:14–17: "And if Christ be not raised, then is our preaching vain, and your faith is also vain."

**Historical section (verified sources):**
- Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 (Church and Brodribb translation, public domain): "Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus." Source: Wikisource, *The Annals (Tacitus)*, Book 15. What it establishes: a hostile non-Christian source records that Jesus was executed under Pontius Pilate. It does not address the resurrection.
- Scope limit: this source supports the execution only. The resurrection claim rests on the apostolic testimony above, which the answer must present as testimony, not as a proof that compels belief.
- Not used: the Josephus *Testimonium* (Antiquities 18.63–64), whose text is disputed. Do not present it as firm evidence.

**Status label:** none. The resurrection is a central Christian confession (Levi, 2026-11-25).

**Still needed before activation:** a second independently checked ancient source, and the Pliny passage (Letters 10.96) with a verified public-domain translation. Not yet fetched.

## 2. Did the early Church believe Jesus is God and the Trinity before Nicaea?

**Scripture (kept, with context):** John 1:1, 14; Matthew 28:19; Acts 5:3–4; add John 20:28 and 2 Corinthians 13:14.

**Pre-Nicene witnesses (ANF vol. 1, public domain; Ante-Nicene Fathers, Schaff/Roberts translation, text at ccel.org):**
- Ignatius, *To the Ephesians* 7 (ANF 1): "...God existing in flesh; true life in death; ... even Jesus Christ our Lord." Witness: the Son is confessed as God. Translation note: the ANF text has its own wording throughout; quote it only as printed.
- Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 6 (ANF 1): "and the Son (who came forth from Him and taught us these things ...), and the prophetic Spirit, we worship and adore." Witness: Son and Spirit are worshipped alongside the Father.
- Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 13 (ANF 1): "...that He is the Son of the true God Himself, and holding Him in the second place, and the prophetic Spirit in the third..." Witness: an early triadic pattern. It is not the later technical formula of one substance, and the answer must say so.

**What this establishes:** that early writers worshipped Christ as God and named the Spirit with the Father and the Son. **What it does not establish:** that they used the later Nicene language in the same way. The Nicene Creed (325) and Chalcedonian Definition (451) gave more precise wording during controversy; they did not invent the biblical teaching. The Fathers are historical witnesses; Scripture is the authority.

**Status label:** none for the Trinity (established orthodoxy, profile).

## 3. Who was Augustine, and is he still important?

**Biography (to verify before activation):** Augustine of Hippo (354–430), born at Thagaste in Roman North Africa; converted and baptised by Ambrose at Milan in 387; ordained presbyter at Hippo Regius in 391 and bishop there c. 395; died in 430 during the Vandal siege of Hippo. These dates are standard, and the CCEL edition's introduction (Outler) gives the baptism, ordination and Thagaste details; check the exact dates against a public-domain biography before release.

**Writings (to verify):** *Confessions* (c. 397–400); *The City of God* (413–426); *On the Trinity* (399–422).

**Verbatim quotation from the *Confessions* (Outler translation, CCEL):** Book I, chapter 1: "...for thou hast made us for thyself and restless is our heart until it comes to rest in thee."

**Importance:** his writings shaped later Western theology, including the Reformers, who read him closely. He lived long before the Reformation and must not be called a Protestant. His writings are historical witnesses and theological arguments to be tested by Scripture, not authorities above it.

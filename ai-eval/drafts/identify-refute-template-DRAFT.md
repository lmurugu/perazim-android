## What the template does
1. **What {group} teaches, in its own words.** Up to three sentences from the approved comparative entry's summary. Any sentence that describes the guide, its method, or "both arguments" is removed.
2. **What Scripture says.** Two or three anchor passages, each quoted word for word from the bundled King James Version. A reference that is not in the KJV is dropped. The answer is refused if fewer than two verses can be quoted (fail closed).
3. **Our biblical answer.** One plain sentence per topic, naming the verses above. PROPOSED wording (below), not yet approved.
4. **Closing.** "We do not judge any person's eternal destiny. Test every teaching by Scripture, as the Bereans did, and talk with a trusted church leader or your pastor."

Only entries marked FALSE_DOCTRINE_EXAMPLE and APPROVED_LEVI are used. Topics covered now: Jehovah's Witnesses, Latter-day Saints, Catholic purgatory, Catholic teaching on Mary. Not yet covered: Orthodox, Seventh-day Adventists, Oneness, Christian Science, canon, Islam.

## Sample 1: Jehovah's Witnesses
```
What Jehovah's Witnesses teaches, in its own words: Jehovah's Witness teaching identifies Jesus with the archangel Michael and holds that Jesus is a created being, not the eternal God the Son. The Jehovah's Witness page 'The Archangel Michael, Who Is He?' says that Michael is 'evidently a name given to Jesus before and after his life on earth.' The Jehovah's Witness Bible Teach book Who Is Jesus Christ? says that Jesus 'was God's first creation' and that 'Jesus is the only one directly created by God'. Historic Christianity, as expressed in the Nicene Creed, confesses the Son as 'begotten, not made', and affirms his eternal deity.

What Scripture says:
John 1:1: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”
Colossians 1:16: “For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him:”
Hebrews 1:6: “And again, when he bringeth in the firstbegotten into the world, he saith, And let all the angels of God worship him.”

Our biblical answer: Scripture says the Word was God and that all things were made by Him, so the Son is not a created being (John 1:1; Colossians 1:16).

We do not judge any person's eternal destiny. Test every teaching by Scripture, as the Bereans did, and talk with a trusted church leader or your pastor.
```

## Sample 2: Latter-day Saints
```
What the Latter-day Saint church teaches, in its own words: Latter-day Saint teaching distinguishes salvation from exaltation. The Church's essay Becoming Like God describes exaltation as the full inheritance of a child of God, available through the Atonement of Jesus Christ and by obedience to the laws and ordinances of the gospel, and says that the power of the Atonement is accessed through faith, repentance, baptism, the gift of the Holy Ghost and enduring to the end. The essay quotes the couplet 'As man now is, God once was: As God now is, man may be', and says that little has been revealed about its first half and that little is taught about it today.

Our biblical answer: Scripture says there is one God, before and after whom none was formed, and that eternal life is God's gift, not a prize earned by exaltation (Isaiah 43:10; Romans 6:23).

We do not judge any person's eternal destiny. Test every teaching by Scripture, as the Bereans did, and talk with a trusted church leader or your pastor.
```

## Sample 3: Catholic purgatory
```
What the Catholic Church teaches, in its own words: Catholic teaching holds that purgatory is a final purification after death for those who die in God's grace and friendship but are still imperfectly purified, so that they may enter the joy of heaven. The Catechism says this purification is different from the punishment of the damned and, in paragraph 1032, appeals to Judas Maccabeus and prayer for the dead. The Council of Florence (1439) and Council of Trent (1563) taught that souls undergoing purification after death are helped by the suffrages of the faithful.

What Scripture says:
1 John 1:7: “But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”
2 Corinthians 5:8: “We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord.”

Our biblical answer: Scripture says the blood of Christ cleanses us from all sin, and that to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord (1 John 1:7; 2 Corinthians 5:8).

We do not judge any person's eternal destiny. Test every teaching by Scripture, as the Bereans did, and talk with a trusted church leader or your pastor.
```

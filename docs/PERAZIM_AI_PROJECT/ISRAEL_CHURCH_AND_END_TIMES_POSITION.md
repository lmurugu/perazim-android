# Israel, the Church and the End Times — Levi's Working Position

**Owner:** Rev. Levi  
**Last consolidated:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** UNDER REVIEW — not a settled Perazim doctrinal statement.  
**Purpose:** Help agents draft consistently without repeatedly asking Levi to restate his position. The exact member-facing Israel/end-times answer remains subject to Levi's final approval.

## 1. The relationship between Israel and the Church

Levi's working position is that there is **one New Covenant people of God in Christ, with Israel and the Church retaining distinct identities**.

Jewish and Gentile believers are united in Christ. Jewish believers are members of the Church. Gentile believers are brought into the blessings of God's promises and are pictured as branches grafted into Israel's olive tree. Gentile believers do not thereby become ethnic Israelites, and the Church does not erase national Israel's identity or mean that God has cancelled His promises to Israel.

Use the biblical picture carefully:

- Romans 11:1–2 — God has not cast away His people.
- Romans 11:17–24 — Gentile believers are grafted in among the natural branches and must not boast over them.
- Romans 11:25–29 — Israel's partial hardening is not the end of God's purpose; God's gifts and calling are irrevocable.
- Ephesians 2:11–16 — Jewish and Gentile believers are brought near and made one in Christ, with the dividing wall broken down.

Avoid both errors: saying the Church has simply replaced Israel, and saying Jewish believers are outside the one Church. The Church is not a Gentile replacement for Israel; Jewish and Gentile believers are united in Christ while national Israel retains a distinct place in Levi's understanding of God's purposes.

A useful explanatory analogy is that Gentile believers are branches grafted into a living tree whose root they did not create. Do not extend an analogy beyond what Romans 11 itself supports.

## 2. The working end-times framework

Levi leans strongly toward:

- A pre-tribulation coming of Christ for His Church.
- A future seven-year tribulation, understood through Daniel's seventieth week.
- A future turning of national Israel to Jesus near the end of the tribulation, after severe trouble and opposition.
- A premillennial return of Christ followed by His physical reign on earth for a thousand years.
- The continuing validity of God's covenant promises concerning Israel, including the land promise.

This is a working interpretation **UNDER REVIEW**. Do not say it is a settled Perazim doctrine or that every faithful Christian must accept every detail. Do not include a catalogue comparing historic premillennialism, amillennialism, postmillennialism and replacement theology unless Levi later asks for it. The ordinary member-facing answer should explain the Perazim working position simply.

## 3. Christ's coming for the Church before the tribulation

Levi's working interpretation is that Christ will come for His Church before the seven-year tribulation. Passages used in the draft include:

- John 14:1–3 — Jesus promises to receive His people to Himself.
- 1 Corinthians 15:51–53 — believers will be changed and the dead raised.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 — the Lord descends, the dead in Christ rise, and living believers are caught up to meet Him.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:9 — believers are not appointed to wrath.
- Revelation 3:10 — the church is promised keeping from the hour of trial.
- Daniel 9:24–27 — the seventy weeks, including the final week, are central to the working seven-year framework.
- Matthew 24:15–22 — Jesus describes severe distress associated with the abomination of desolation.

Important distinction: the phrase "pre-tribulation rapture" is not a direct quotation from these texts. It is Levi's interpretation of how multiple passages fit together. The seven-year tribulation is likewise an interpretation of Daniel's final week and its relation to other prophetic passages, not a phrase that should be attributed directly to one isolated verse.

When explaining the view, distinguish the explicit statements of each passage from the sequence inferred by reading them together. Never set dates.

## 4. Christ's thousand-year reign

Levi's working position is premillennial: Christ returns before the thousand years and physically reigns on earth.

Passages relevant to the position include:

- Revelation 19:11–16 — Christ appears as the victorious King.
- Revelation 20:1–6 — Satan is bound and the saints reign with Christ for a thousand years. This is where the thousand-year period is stated explicitly.
- Zechariah 14:4, 9 — the Lord's intervention and His kingship over all the earth.
- Isaiah 11:1–10 — the righteous ruler from Jesse's line and the character of His reign.

Do not claim that every detail appears in every passage. Revelation 20 explicitly gives the thousand years; the other passages are used to explain the Messiah's righteous earthly reign. State this as Levi's working interpretation, not an uncontested formulation accepted by all Christians.

## 5. The future turning of national Israel to Christ

Levi's working understanding is that national Israel will turn to Jesus near the end of the tribulation, following a time of severe trouble and opposition.

Relevant passages used in the developing framework include:

- Daniel 9:24–27 — the seventy weeks and final week.
- Daniel 12:1 — a time of unparalleled trouble for Daniel's people.
- Matthew 24:15–22 — severe distress associated with the abomination of desolation.
- Zechariah 12:10 — the people look upon the One they pierced and mourn.
- Romans 11:25–27 — Israel's partial hardening and the promise that all Israel will be saved.
- 2 Thessalonians 2:1–4 — the falling away and the man of sin who opposes God.
- Revelation 13:1–8 — the beast's opposition and authority, used in the wider interpretation of the final opponent of Christ.

The sequence connecting these passages is Levi's interpretation, not a single chronological statement explicitly written in one passage. Keep it labelled **UNDER REVIEW**. Do not identify a present-day person, government or nation as the Antichrist. Do not speculate about dates.

## 6. The Antichrist

Levi understands the prophetic passages as pointing to a final individual opponent of Christ near the end. Relevant passages include:

- 1 John 2:18 — many antichrists have appeared, and Scripture speaks of the antichrist.
- Daniel 7:8, 25 — the arrogant ruler who speaks against the Most High and oppresses the saints.
- 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4 — the man of sin who opposes God and exalts himself.
- Revelation 13:1–8 — the beast and his opposition to God and His people.
- Matthew 24:36 — no one knows the day or hour.

Explain what each text actually says before connecting them. Do not collapse every reference to "antichrist", "man of sin" and "beast" into one label without explaining that this is an interpretive connection. Never name a current individual as the Antichrist or set a date for Christ's return.

## 7. The land promise

Levi believes God's covenant promises concerning Israel's land have not been cancelled. The working explanation must acknowledge both historical fulfilment and future expectation.

Use these passages with their proper distinctions:

- Genesis 15:18–21 — God's promise of land to Abraham's descendants.
- Genesis 17:7–8 — the covenant and the land of Canaan as an everlasting possession.
- Joshua 21:43 — Scripture records that the Lord gave Israel the land sworn to their fathers and that they took possession of it. Do not ignore this historical fulfilment.
- Ezekiel 36:24–28 — the promise to gather Israel from the nations, bring them into their land and give them a new heart and spirit.
- Ezekiel 37:21–28 — the promise to gather Israel, make them one nation in the land and establish one King over them.
- Romans 11:25–29 — God's continuing purpose for Israel and the irrevocability of His gifts and calling.

Romans 11 supports the wider claim of God's continuing purpose, but must not be used alone as proof of the land promise.

This is a covenant and biblical interpretation. It does not mean that every action, policy or political decision of the modern State of Israel is automatically approved by God. Do not turn a theological promise into blanket political endorsement or hostility toward other peoples.

## 8. Wording and teaching rules

- Use the phrases "our working interpretation" and "under review" whenever presenting an inference or the end-times framework.
- Mark what Scripture says directly separately from what Levi understands when several passages are read together.
- Explain words such as "tribulation", "rapture", "grafted", "premillennial" and "Antichrist" in everyday language.
- Use multiple relevant passages; do not build the teaching on one isolated verse.
- Do not list competing end-times systems in the ordinary answer.
- Do not identify a present-day Antichrist or set dates.
- Do not imply that all modern Israeli government actions are approved by God.
- Do not pronounce on any person's salvation.
- Keep the answer warm, concise and clear enough for an ordinary church member.

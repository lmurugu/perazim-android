## Purpose and method

This report records the supporting evidence added, the claims revised, and the limits that remain for the 23 entries which were pending after the four earlier approvals. The objective was to make each model-facing statement no broader than the evidence permits. The initial report was created alongside KB 1.5.11; subsequent precision, attribution and source-order corrections were integrated through KB 1.5.15. The live JSON at PR head is authoritative if any wording in this historical report differs from it.

The source hierarchy used was: (1) primary religious texts and official institutional statements; (2) official educational explanations and confessions; (3) reputable academic reference works and peer-reviewed research; and (4) secondary historical summaries, used only when clearly labelled as such. An inaccessible primary source was not treated as verified. Instead, claims were either corroborated by independent scholarship, attributed to the source actually read, narrowed, or removed.

No entry has been promoted to `APPROVED_LEVI` in this revision. The four previously authorised approvals are preserved. The remaining 23 entries are revised drafts for owner review.

## A. Indian religions, rebirth and pluralism

### 1. `karma`

**Revised description.** The entry now treats Hindu, Buddhist and Jain karma as related but different teachings. It describes Buddhist karma with intention at its centre and causal continuity without an eternal self. The Jain account of karma as subtle matter affecting the jīva is expressly attributed to Jain philosophy and specialist scholarship, not presented as an interchangeable Hindu or Buddhist doctrine.

**Evidence added or checked.**
- [Bhagavad Gita, trans. K. T. Telang, Sacred Books of the East 8 (1882)](https://archive.org/details/bhagavadgtwithsa00tela), especially 2.47 and 18.12, for action and attachment to its fruit.
- [Anguttara Nikaya 6.63, trans. Bhikkhu Sujato](https://suttacentral.net/an6.63/en/sujato), for intention as deed and karmic consequence.
- [Acaranga Sutra, Book 1, Lecture 1, trans. Hermann Jacobi](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sacred_Books_of_the_East/Volume_22/%C3%82k%C3%A2r%C3%A2%E1%B9%85ga_S%C3%BBtra/Book_1/Lecture_1), for early Jain teaching on soul and action.
- Marie-Hélène Gorisse, [“Jaina Philosophy,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaina-philosophy/), especially sections 1.3 and 2.4.
- Ana Bajželj, [“Self and Matter: Jaina Non-one-sided Dualism,” *Journal of Indian Philosophy* 54 (2026), 341–360](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10781-026-09641-2). The open-access article examines Pūjyapāda and Akalaṅka's commentaries on the Tattvārtha-sūtra and corroborates the relationship between karmic matter and the jīva.

**Limit.** Jain philosophers differ in the precise account of the self's relation to karmic matter. The text therefore says “Jain teaching” and attributes the specific technical explanation to scholarly sources. Galatians 6:7–8 is explicitly distinguished from rebirth-based karma; biblical sowing and reaping is not equated with karma.

The entry also notes that some Buddhists reject rebirth, avoiding a claim that every Buddhist school explains it identically.

**Evidence.**
- Bhagavad Gita 2.20 and 2.22, Telang translation, for Hindu teaching on the continuing self and successive bodies.

**Limit.** School differences remain; the entry no longer implies that Hindu, Jain and Buddhist accounts are one doctrine.

# Israel and the end times: revised draft for Levi's review (2026-11-25)

## 1. Israel and the Church

Our working position is that the Church has not replaced Israel. Jewish and Gentile believers are united in Christ as one people of God. This does not mean that Gentile believers have become ethnic Israel, and it does not mean that Israel has lost its distinct identity.

- God has not rejected His people. Paul says so directly: "God hath not cast away his people which he foreknew" (Romans 11:1-2 KJV).
- Gentile believers are grafted in, not replacing the tree. "wert graffed in among them" (Romans 11:17, KJV). The root is the promises to the patriarchs, and the branches are both Jew and Gentile who believe.
- In Christ, the wall is broken down. Christ "hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition" (Ephesians 2:14-16). Jew and Gentile are one new people, not one people with the Jewish people removed.
- Paul warns the Gentile believer not to boast against the natural branches (Romans 11:18-21), and says the gifts and calling of God are "without repentance" (Romans 11:29).

Plain summary for a member: God has one family of believers, made up of Jews and Gentiles in Christ. Gentile Christians are not Israel. God's promises to Israel stand.

## 2. Our working view of the future of Israel and the Church

Our working interpretation is that national Israel has a future turning to the Lord. Paul writes that "blindness in part is happened to Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in. And so all Israel shall be saved" (Romans 11:25-26 KJV).

- What Scripture explicitly says: the partial hardening of Israel is for a time, and the turning of Israel is promised (Romans 11:25-26).
- Our interpretation: that "all Israel" means national Israel turning to Christ in a future day, not a spiritual group only.
- We do not claim that every Christian agrees with this reading. Many faithful believers read these verses differently. The guide must say so.

## 3. The pre-tribulation view (our working interpretation)

We believe Christ will come for His Church before the tribulation. Scripture gives the basis for this view in these passages:

- Christ's coming for His people: "the Lord himself shall descend from heaven... and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them" (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17).
- The Church is not appointed to God's wrath: "God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Thessalonians 5:9).
- The promise to keep the faithful from the hour of trial: "I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world" (Revelation 3:10).

The guide must present this as our working interpretation. It must say that the view is still under review, that it is not a settled Perazim doctrine, and that other believers hold different views. It must not present a list of competing views as a catalogue.

## 4. The thousand-year reign (our working interpretation)

Scripture explicitly says that Christ returns in glory to judge and to rule. "I saw heaven opened, and behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True... and his name is called The Word of God... King of kings, and Lord of lords" (Revelation 19:11-16).

Revelation 20:1-6 describes Satan bound for a thousand years, and the saints reigning with Christ. Our working interpretation is that this is a literal future reign of Christ on earth, with the saints reigning with Him. This is how we read these two passages together. It is not a point on which all Christians agree, and the guide must say that.

## 5. The antichrist

What Scripture explicitly says:

- There will be a man of lawlessness who opposes God and exalts himself (2 Thessalonians 2:3-4).
- Antichrist spirit and antichrists already exist in the world (1 John 2:18; 1 John 4:3).
- Nobody knows the day or hour of Christ's return (Matthew 24:36). The guide must not set dates.

Our interpretation: how these passages fit together into a sequence of events. This is our reading, and not something Scripture states outright.

The guide must not speculate about the identity of the antichrist, must not name any person or country, and must not set dates or periods.

## 6. The land promise

What Scripture explicitly says:

- God made a covenant with Abraham: "Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates" (Genesis 15:18-21 KJV).
- The LORD fulfilled that promise in the time of Joshua: "And the LORD gave unto Israel all the land which he sware to give unto their fathers... there failed not ought of any good thing which the LORD had spoken" (Joshua 21:43-45 KJV).
- God's gifts and calling to Israel are not cancelled: "For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance" (Romans 11:29 KJV).

The guide must acknowledge the historical fulfilment recorded in Joshua. It must explain our working belief that God's promises to Israel have not been cancelled, and that the future turning of Israel is part of that faithfulness (Romans 11:25-29). It must say that this is our interpretation, not a settled doctrine.

## 7. What was removed

The earlier draft compared historic premillennialism, amillennialism, postmillennialism and replacement theology. That section is removed. The member needs to understand our biblical position, not a catalogue of competing positions. If a member asks about another view, the guide answers from Scripture and says it is not the Perazim working position. It does not describe the other view as equally valid.

## 8. Tone

Keep the answer warm and simple. Use everyday language, explain each passage in a sentence or two, and end with what it means for a believer today: trust God's faithfulness, love Israel and the Church alike, pray for the peace of Jerusalem (Psalm 122:6), and keep watch without setting dates (Matthew 24:42).

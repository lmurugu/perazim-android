## 1. Biblical passages and original context

- 1 Timothy 2:11–15: women to learn in quietness; a prohibition on teaching or exercising authority over a man; the appeal to creation and Eve.
- 1 Timothy 3:1–7: qualifications for an overseer, including "the husband of one wife" (*anēr*).
- Titus 1:5–9: elders, with similar qualifications.
- Romans 16:1–7: Phoebe as *diakonos* and *prostatis*; Junia described as "outstanding among the apostles" (the name's form is debated).
- Acts 18:24–26: Priscilla and Aquila explain the way of God to Apollos.
- Acts 2:17–18 (Joel quoted): sons and daughters prophesy.
- Acts 21:8–9: Philip's four prophesying daughters.
- Galatians 3:28: "there is neither male nor female" in Christ.
- 1 Corinthians 11:2–16: head coverings and prayer or prophecy by women.
- 1 Corinthians 14:33–36: women to keep silent in the churches.
- Ephesians 5:21–33: headship and mutual submission in marriage.
- Judges 4–5 (Deborah) and Matthew 28:1–10 (women as first witnesses of the resurrection).

## 2. Terms that affect interpretation

- Greek *authenteō* (1 Timothy 2:12, "usurp authority" or "exercise authority"). Its meaning is debated.
- *Hēsychía* (quietness, 1 Timothy 2:11–12) and *didáskein* (to teach).
- *Anēr* and *gynḗ* (man and woman, or husband and wife, depending on context).
- *Diákonos* (servant, deacon, minister), *presbýteros* (elder), *episkopos* (overseer).
- *Prostatis* (Romans 16:2): helper, leader, or patron.
- *Ḥelpâ kenegdô* (Genesis 2:18): a helper corresponding to him.

## 3. Wider biblical teaching

Scripture affirms that men and women are equal in dignity and in salvation, and that both receive the Spirit and gifts (Galatians 3:28; Acts 2:17–18; 1 Corinthians 12:7–11). It also addresses order, gifting and roles within the church and marriage. The relation between these is interpreted differently.

## 4. Historical Christian interpretations

Most of church history limited ordained leadership and teaching authority to men. Some movements from the 19th century onward allowed women to preach and lead. Many churches permit women to teach and preach but not to serve as senior pastors or elders. Historical detail is to be confirmed before this section is finalised.

## 5. Competing evangelical positions

1. **Complementarian (restrictive):** eldership and pastoral oversight are reserved for men. Women may serve in other gifts, including teaching other women and children.
2. **Intermediate (functional):** women may preach and teach in the congregation, but eldership and the overseer office are reserved for men.
3. **Egalitarian (open):** all ministry offices and gifts are open to women and men on the basis of gifting and calling. Restrictive texts are read in their original setting.
4. **Mixed or local:** practice varies by setting, with a stated principle.

The distinctions must be kept clear: preaching, teaching, pastoral oversight, eldership, deaconship and other ministry roles are not the same thing.

## 7. Pastoral implications and exceptions

- Women and men who sense a call to ministry are treated with respect and referred to the church's authorised leaders for discernment.
- The distinction between ability or gifting and ecclesiastical appointment is explained, not blurred.
- Disagreement about this matter is handled with charity. Members must not be pressured.

## 8. What the AI may and may not say

**May:**
- explain the passages, terms and the four views fairly;
- distinguish preaching, teaching, oversight, eldership and diaconate;
- say that the church has not yet adopted a position.

**May not:**
- say "Perazim permits women to be pastors" or "Perazim prohibits women from preaching";
- tell a woman whether she may hold a particular ministry role in the church;
- present any single view as the only faithful reading.

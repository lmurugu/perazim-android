## 1. Biblical passages and original context

- Genesis 9:20–21: Noah's wine and drunkenness.
- Genesis 19:30–38: Lot's daughters' use of wine. A narrative, not a teaching on drink.
- Numbers 6:1–4: the Nazirite vow to abstain from wine and strong drink.
- Psalm 104:14–15: wine that gladdens the heart.
- Proverbs 20:1; 23:29–35; 31:4–7: warnings against drunkenness, and wine for those in distress.
- Isaiah 5:11–12, 22: woe to those who pursue strong drink.
- John 2:1–11: Jesus' wine at Cana.
- Matthew 11:19: Jesus accused of being a "winebibber".
- Luke 1:15: John the Baptist (the angel's word about wine and strong drink).
- Romans 14:19–23; 1 Corinthians 8:7–13: Christian liberty and the weaker brother.
- Ephesians 5:18: "be not drunk with wine".
- 1 Timothy 3:3, 8; Titus 1:7: overseers and deacons not given to wine (*paroinos*). 1 Timothy 5:23: Timothy told to use a little wine for his stomach.

## 2. Terms that affect interpretation

- Hebrew *yayin* (wine), *tirōš* (new wine, grape juice or fermented wine, debated), *šēkār* (strong drink).
- Greek *oinos* (wine), *methē* (drunkenness), *methuō* (to be drunk), *paroinos* (given to wine).
- Whether the New Testament distinguishes fermented wine from unfermented juice is debated. Scholars and churches disagree.

## 3. Wider biblical teaching

Scripture permits wine as a blessing and condemns drunkenness, excess and the harm it does. It emphasises self-control, love for others and the good of the weak.

## 4. Historical Christian interpretations

Most historic churches have permitted moderate use and condemned drunkenness. Some movements in the 18th and 19th centuries promoted total abstinence. Views on the grape-juice question vary.

## 5. Competing evangelical positions

1. Total abstinence as the faithful standard, for the sake of the weak and because of addiction.
2. Moderate use is permitted, drunkenness is forbidden, and public and church practice is shaped by love for others.
3. Abstinence as wise witness in some contexts, while moderate use remains a matter of liberty.
4. Interpretation of "wine" as partly or wholly unfermented. Contested.

## 7. Pastoral implications and exceptions

- Addiction is a serious health and spiritual concern. Refer to NACADA on **1192** and to treatment (G03).
- Members in recovery deserve support and respect for their decision to abstain.
- Church teaching must consider the weak brother and public witness.
- Drinking in the presence of minors, or drinking and driving, is a safety concern.

## 8. What the AI may and may not say

**May:** explain the passages, terms and views fairly; describe the harm of drunkenness and addiction; give NACADA 1192 where dependency is mentioned.

**May not:** state Perazim's position; endorse drunkenness; tell a member whether they may drink; minimise addiction.

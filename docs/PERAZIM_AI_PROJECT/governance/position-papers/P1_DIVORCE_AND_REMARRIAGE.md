## 1. Biblical passages and original context

- Deuteronomy 24:1–4: a certificate of divorce for a husband who finds "some uncleanness" in his wife. Law regulating a practice, not a command to divorce.
- Malachi 2:13–16: judgement on those who deal treacherously with the wife of their youth, and the statement that God "hates putting away".
- Matthew 5:31–32 and Matthew 19:3–12: Jesus on divorce, with the exception clause "except for sexual immorality" (porneia) in Matthew.
- Mark 10:2–12 and Luke 16:18: Jesus' teaching without the exception clause.
- 1 Corinthians 7:10–16: Paul on separation and an unbelieving spouse who departs.
- Romans 7:1–3: marriage as binding while a spouse lives.

## 2. Terms that affect interpretation

- *Porneia* (Matthew 5:32; 19:9): broad sexual immorality, or a narrower sense such as marital unfaithfulness. Scholars differ.
- *Chōrizō* (1 Corinthians 7:10–15): to separate. Used for separation of a believer from an unbelieving spouse who wants to leave.
- *Apoluō* and *aphiēmi* (Matthew 19:9; 1 Corinthians 7:11–13): to send away, to release.
- Hebrew *'ervat dābār* (Deuteronomy 24:1): "some uncleanness", interpreted in several ways.
- Hebrew *śānēʾ* (Malachi 2:16): to hate, often read as a strong negative about divorce.

## 3. Wider biblical teaching

Scripture presents marriage as a covenant (Malachi 2:14; Genesis 2:24). It also recognises that marriages break, and addresses faithfulness, repentance and God's grace to the broken.

## 4. Historical Christian interpretations

Early and medieval writers differed on the exception clauses and on remarriage. Some permitted divorce but not remarriage. Others allowed remarriage after adultery. The Reformation churches also differed. Catholic teaching has permitted separation without remarriage while both spouses live. The details require historical research before this section is finalised.

## 5. Competing evangelical positions

1. No divorce, only separation, with remarriage never permitted while a former spouse lives.
2. Divorce permitted for sexual immorality, with remarriage permitted for the innocent party.
3. Divorce permitted for sexual immorality and for desertion by an unbelieving spouse (Paul's concession).
4. Additional grounds, such as serious abuse or persistent abandonment, argued from principle and pastoral care. This view is contested.
5. A restoration-centred pastoral approach that focuses on repentance, reconciliation where possible, and support for the wronged and the divorced.

## 7. Pastoral implications and exceptions

- Safety comes first. No one is counselled to remain in an abusive or dangerous marriage. Refer to safeguarding and to 1195 (G03).
- Separated and divorced members receive compassion and privacy, not judgement.
- Marriage breakdown involving church leaders is referred to authorised pastoral and safeguarding personnel (G03 section 5).
- Church discipline and pastoral process belong to the church's authorised leaders, not the AI.

## 8. What the AI may and may not say

**May:**
- explain the passages, terms and the main views fairly;
- say that the church has not yet adopted a position on this matter;
- encourage the member to speak with a pastor and to seek safety where needed;
- give the crisis directory contacts for abuse (1195, 116 where a child is involved).

**May not:**
- state that Perazim permits or forbids divorce or remarriage;
- tell a person whether they are free to remarry, or give a verdict on their marriage;
- advise a person to stay in an abusive marriage;
- decide any individual's case.

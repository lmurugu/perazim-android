## 1. Biblical passages and original context

- Genesis 14:18–20: Abraham gives a tenth of spoils to Melchizedek.
- Genesis 28:20–22: Jacob's vow to give a tenth.
- Leviticus 27:30–32: the tithe of land and herd belongs to the LORD.
- Numbers 18:21–24: the Levites receive the tithe for their service.
- Deuteronomy 14:22–29: the tithe eaten before the LORD, and every third year's tithe for the needy.
- Malachi 3:8–10: "bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse".
- Matthew 23:23 and Luke 11:42: Jesus on tithing mint, rue and herbs without neglecting justice and mercy.
- Luke 18:12: the Pharisee's tithing.
- Hebrews 7:1–10: Melchizedek and Abraham's tithe.
- Acts 2:44–45 and 4:32–37: shared resources in the early church.
- 1 Corinthians 16:1–2 and 2 Corinthians 8–9: regular giving, cheerful and proportionate giving.

## 2. Terms that affect interpretation

- Hebrew *maʿaśēr* (tithe, "a tenth"). Greek *dekatē* (Hebrews 7:2).
- *Hilarós* (2 Corinthians 9:7): cheerful giver.
- *Kathṓs euodṓtai* (1 Corinthians 16:2): "as he may prosper", often read as proportionate giving.
- Whether "tithe" in the New Testament refers to a fixed legal obligation or a general principle is debated.

## 3. Wider biblical teaching

Scripture consistently treats giving as worship and trust, and condemns exploitation of the poor and misuse of offerings. Generosity is central in the New Testament, which emphasises the heart of the giver.

## 4. Historical Christian interpretations

The church has long taught that the tithe was a mandatory Old Covenant obligation. Medieval Western churches enforced tithes as a legal duty. The Reformation churches differed on whether tithing binds Christians. Many modern churches teach tithing as a helpful starting principle.

## 5. Competing evangelical positions

1. Tithing (a tenth) is an ongoing command for Christians, the New Covenant continuing the principle.
2. The tithe is a helpful minimum or starting point, with generosity beyond it.
3. The tithe is part of the Mosaic law, fulfilled in Christ. Christian giving is free and proportionate, not a fixed percentage.
4. Giving is measured by the heart, need and the church's mission, with no fixed percentage.

## 7. Pastoral implications and exceptions

- Members in poverty must never be told that they are withholding God's blessing or are under a curse.
- Prosperity teaching that links giving to guaranteed financial return is not endorsed and must be explained honestly.
- Financial exploitation by any leader is a safeguarding matter (G03 section 5).
- Decisions on a member's giving are personal and before God. The AI does not set amounts.

## 8. What the AI may and may not say

**May:** explain the passages, the terms and the views fairly; explain how the church receives and uses giving once an approved financial communication exists (G06); encourage generosity and stewardship.

**May not:** state Perazim's position; tell a member how much to give; promise financial returns for giving; present a giving instruction that is not from an approved financial communication.

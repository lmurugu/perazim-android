## 2. Notes per contact

**922.** Reported by Citizen Digital (6 Aug 2026) and AllAfrica (6 Aug 2026, Capital FM reprint) as the national emergency medical number, launched on 5 August 2026 with the SHA National Ambulance Dispatch Centre. The claim that calls are free on all networks appears only in the AllAfrica report, quoting the President. The Capital FM original would not load. Before display: find an official government or SHA page, and test calls on each major network.

**999 / 112 / 911.** The Police Service's own Service Standards PDF lists 999/112/911 as police hotlines. The Standard newspaper list (2017) was **not** used as a source because it is dated and is a writer's list.

**Befrienders Kenya.** Befrienders Worldwide's page lists +254 722 178 177 and says both "Monday to Sunday, 9:00 to 17:00" and "Open 24 hours a day". Find A Helpline lists "Monday to Friday, 9 am to 5 pm" and channels of phone, SMS and WhatsApp. The sources conflict. The Guide must not describe this line as 24-hour, and must always state that it is not an emergency service. The email address in the proposal was **not checked** and is excluded.

**1195.** UN Women's October 2024 article describes the national GBV toll-free line, 24/7, with HAK. Current operation must be checked, and a live call made.

**116.** MIGECAH (government) describes a free 24/7 child helpline with WhatsApp 0722 116 116. Child Helpline International gives the WhatsApp number as +254 722 116 116, which is the same number. Operated as a public-private partnership between the government and Childline Kenya.

**1192.** NACADA's own site says the line is free, and is available 24 hours every day. The office line +254 202721997 is **not** the helpline.

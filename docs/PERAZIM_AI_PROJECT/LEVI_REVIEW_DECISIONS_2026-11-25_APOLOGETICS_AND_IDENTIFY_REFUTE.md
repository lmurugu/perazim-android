## 1. General instructions

- Scripture is the first and final authority. Use at least two relevant passages in context when establishing a doctrine. Do not use an isolated verse as the sole basis for a teaching.
- Distinguish biblical teaching, historical evidence, logical inference and a developing Perazim interpretation.
- Do not label every apologetic inference "working interpretation, under review." Reserve that label for positions Levi has actually marked UNDER REVIEW, such as unconditional election, apostasy/assurance formulation and the detailed end-times framework.
- The resurrection, Trinity, inspiration of Scripture, existence of God and gospel are not to be presented as undecided merely because they require explanation.
- Historical claims must be supported by sources actually checked. A historical source may support a historical fact; it is never an authority above Scripture for doctrine.
- Member-facing answers must be written in understandable everyday English.

## 2. Review of the nine apologetics outlines

### 1. Did Jesus rise from the dead? — REVISE BEFORE ACTIVATION

The biblical foundation is good: 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, Acts 1:3 and John 20:24–29 give early Christian testimony and resurrection appearances. Keep the passages.

However, the question promises historical apologetics, while the outline currently contains almost no evaluation of historical evidence outside the biblical testimony. Add a concise historical section based on sources actually verified. Explain what the earliest Christian testimony establishes and what other ancient sources can establish. For example, an ancient reference to Jesus' execution or early Christian worship is not proof of the resurrection itself; do not overstate what Tacitus or Pliny establish. Do not use a disputed form of Josephus' Testimonium as an uncontested quotation.

Remove the label "working interpretation, under review" from the resurrection itself. Christ's bodily resurrection is a central Christian confession, not one of Levi's developing doctrinal positions. Explain that if Christ has not been raised, Christian faith is empty (1 Corinthians 15:14–17), and that the apostolic testimony is central to the Christian claim.

### 2. Can we trust the Bible we have? — REVISE BEFORE ACTIVATION

Separate four questions that the current draft blends together:

1. **Inspiration:** Scripture is God-breathed (2 Timothy 3:16) and prophecy came as men were moved by the Holy Spirit (2 Peter 1:20–21).
2. **Eyewitness and historical reporting:** Luke 1:1–4 says Luke investigated earlier accounts and eyewitness testimony.
3. **Preservation and manuscript transmission:** explain how the text has been transmitted, what manuscript copies and textual differences mean, and why variants do not automatically destroy the message. This needs verified historical/textual sources, not only proof texts.
4. **Canon:** do not use this answer to claim that Levi has approved the app's 66-book canon formulation; the doctrine register currently marks that entry DRAFT_PENDING_LEVI.

Remove the claim that John 14:26 teaches the Holy Spirit guards the truth of the entire Bible in every believer's life. In its immediate setting, Jesus promises the Spirit's teaching and remembrance to His disciples. Do not make it a direct proof of later manuscript copying or canon recognition.

The sentence "God's word is trustworthy as the church receives it" is too vague. State precisely what is meant and support the historical claims with checked sources. Do not promise that every textual question is simple or that no textual variants exist.

### 3. Did the early Church believe Jesus is God and the Trinity before Nicaea? — REVISE BEFORE ACTIVATION

The biblical passages are relevant: John 1:1, 14; Matthew 28:19; Acts 5:3–4. Add other relevant passages where needed, such as John 20:28 and 2 Corinthians 13:14, with context.

The question asks about belief before Nicaea. The Nicene Creed (325) and Chalcedonian Definition (451) cannot by themselves demonstrate what Christians believed before those councils. Add verified pre-Nicene primary witnesses, with author, work, approximate date and exact section. Possible primary sources for research include Ignatius of Antioch, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus and Tertullian. Check the actual passage and its context; do not rely on a quotation website or a later paraphrase.

Explain simply that the councils did not create Jesus' deity or invent the biblical teaching; they gave more precise wording to defend the Church's confession during controversy. Do not claim that every pre-Nicene writer used the later technical language in exactly the same way. The Church Fathers are supporting historical witnesses only; Scripture remains the authority.

Remove "working interpretation, under review" from the Trinity itself. The Nicene and Chalcedonian formulations are recorded as established orthodoxy in Levi's doctrine register.

### 4. Does God exist? — APPROVE THE BIBLICAL DIRECTION; MAKE MINOR EDITS

Romans 1:19–20 and Psalm 19:1–4 are appropriate foundations for the claim that creation points to its Creator. Romans 2:14–15 can support a moral argument.

Do not use Hebrews 11:6 as though it independently proves God's existence to a sceptic; it describes the necessity of faith and what faith believes. It may be used to explain the Christian response.

Explain the reasoning in ordinary language: the existence and order of the world point beyond themselves to a Creator; human moral awareness raises the question of a moral Lawgiver. Present this as a reasoned case, not a claim that every question is thereby settled or that every person will be compelled to believe.

Remove "working interpretation, under review." This is an apologetic explanation of biblical claims, not a pending Perazim doctrine.

### 5. Why does evil exist if God is all-powerful and all-loving? — REVISE BEFORE ACTIVATION

Keep James 1:13, Genesis 50:20 and Romans 8:28, but explain their contexts. Romans 8:28 is a promise to those who love God; it does not mean every event is good in itself. Genesis 50:20 describes God's providence in Joseph's particular story; it illustrates but does not by itself answer every question about evil.

Add Genesis 3 to explain human rebellion and the entrance of sin into human life; Acts 2:22–24 to show human responsibility alongside God's saving purpose in the crucifixion; Romans 8:18–25 and Revelation 21:1–5 to explain future redemption and the end of suffering.

Explain that God does not commit evil or call evil good. Human beings remain responsible for evil choices. God can work out His purposes even through evil without excusing the evil. We do not know why God permits every particular act of suffering, and the AI must not give a glib answer to a grieving person. Keep the cross and future justice central.

Remove "working interpretation, under review" unless a sentence states a specific developing position that Levi has not settled.

### 6. Do Christians disagree, so Christianity is false? — APPROVE THE DIRECTION; MAKE MINOR EDITS

Keep Ephesians 4:3–6, 1 Corinthians 1:10 and Acts 17:11, but explain what each passage is doing. The command to unity does not mean Christians have never disagreed.

Add 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 or another suitable passage to identify the central gospel claims, and Galatians 1:8–9 to show that not every teaching is acceptable. Explain that disagreement by itself does not prove Christianity false; disagreements must be examined and tested. Some are about secondary matters; others affect central truths.

Do not imply every disagreement is harmless or that all denominations teach the same thing. Do not label this answer "working interpretation, under review."

### 7. Does the Bible contradict itself? — REVISE BEFORE ACTIVATION

Psalm 119:160 and John 17:17 state the Bible's truthfulness, but simply quoting them does not answer a specific alleged contradiction. Avoid circular reasoning.

Explain the method: identify the exact passages, read their context, check who is speaking and what each author is addressing, distinguish different events or perspectives, and check translation or manuscript issues where relevant. Do not assume every alleged contradiction is resolved before examining it.

A general answer may state the Christian conviction that Scripture is true and invite the user to provide the two passages. The AI must then examine that specific example rather than give a stock refusal. Do not claim that every difficult passage has already been explained.

Remove "working interpretation, under review" from the general doctrine of Scripture's truthfulness.

### 8. Is faith believing without evidence? — APPROVE THE DIRECTION; MAKE MINOR EDITS

Hebrews 11:1, Romans 10:17 and Acts 17:31 are relevant. Add John 20:30–31 or 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 to show that the Gospel writers and apostles presented testimony intended to produce belief.

Explain that biblical faith is trust in God based on His word and what He has revealed and done; it is not pretending to know something without any reason. Also explain that faith includes trust and commitment, not merely agreeing that a fact is true.

Do not imply that every spiritual truth must be demonstrated by a laboratory experiment. Remove "working interpretation, under review."

### 9. Who was Augustine, and is he still important? — REWRITE BEFORE ACTIVATION

The current draft does not answer the question. "Scripture gives no biography of later teachers" is not a sufficient answer.

Give a short, accurate biography of Augustine of Hippo (354–430), explaining that he was a Christian bishop and influential theologian in Roman North Africa. Identify a few important writings, such as *Confessions*, *The City of God* and *On the Trinity*, and explain briefly why later Christians, including Protestant Reformers, have engaged his work.

Use verified historical sources and, where useful, Augustine's own writings. Do not call Augustine a Protestant; he lived many centuries before the Reformation. Explain that his writings are historical witnesses and theological arguments to be tested by Scripture, not authorities above it. Do not turn his every opinion into Perazim doctrine.

## 3. Identify-and-refute template — APPROVE THE STRUCTURE; REVISE EXAMPLES BEFORE ENABLEMENT

The four-part structure is approved in principle:

1. What the group teaches.
2. What Scripture says.
3. Why the teaching does not agree with Scripture.
4. A respectful closing that does not judge an individual's eternal destiny.

Make the following changes:

- Replace "What {group} teaches, in its own words" with **"What this group teaches"** unless the text is an exact quotation. A paraphrase must never be presented as a group's own words.
- The group's official source may be used to verify the group's claim. It is evidence of what the group teaches, not an authority for Christian doctrine. Keep it distinct from the Bible evidence.
- Use at least two relevant KJV passages in context. Quote from the verified bundled text only. Do not let the model reconstruct quotations from memory.
- Explain in plain language why the teaching conflicts with Scripture. Do not insult or condemn every member of the group.
- The closing may say: "We do not judge any person's standing before God. Test every teaching by Scripture, and speak with a trusted church leader if you need help."

### Jehovah's Witnesses — APPROVE THE SUBJECT; STRENGTHEN THE BIBLICAL ANSWER

The current summary is acceptable if it is verified against the official Jehovah's Witness sources and is correctly attributed. Remove the Nicene Creed from the paragraph describing what Jehovah's Witnesses teach; it is not their teaching.

Use John 1:1, John 1:3 and Hebrews 1:8–12 (or the relevant verses within that passage) to show the Word's deity and that all created things came into being through Him. Keep the KJV wording exact. The conclusion must not depend on Colossians 1:16 alone.

### Latter-day Saints — REWRITE THE REFUTATION

Represent the distinction between salvation and exaltation accurately. Do not say simply that Latter-day Saints believe eternal life is earned by works; their official teaching also appeals to Christ's Atonement, faith and grace.

Use Isaiah 43:10, Isaiah 44:6–8 and 1 Timothy 6:15–16 to explain the Bible's teaching that the LORD alone is God. Use Ephesians 2:8–10 and Romans 6:23, where relevant, to explain salvation as God's gift and good works as its fruit. Do not claim that these two ideas alone refute every aspect of the LDS account. Explain the specific disagreement: biblical creatures do not become additional gods alongside the one eternal God.

### Catholic purgatory — REWRITE THE REFUTATION

Represent Catholic teaching as it actually defines purgatory: a final purification of those who die in God's grace, not a second sacrifice or the punishment of the damned.

Then state the Protestant biblical conclusion: Christ's one offering is sufficient (Hebrews 10:10–14); His blood cleanses from sin (1 John 1:7); and the believer's hope after death is to be with Christ (Luke 23:43; Philippians 1:23; 2 Corinthians 5:6–8). Explain that we find no biblical teaching requiring a further purifying state after death. Do not pretend that one verse alone settles the debate or misstate the Catholic doctrine.

### Catholic teaching about Mary — NARROW THE SUBJECT AND REWRITE

Do not use the broad phrase "Catholic teaching on Mary" as if all Marian teaching were one claim. Identify the exact doctrine being examined, such as invocation of Mary or a claimed special mediating role.

Represent Catholic teaching accurately: Catholic explanations distinguish Mary's subordinate intercession from Christ's unique mediation. Do not claim Catholics teach that Mary is equal to Christ or an independent saviour.

Use 1 Timothy 2:1–6 in its full context: Paul commands prayer and intercession for others while affirming Christ as the one mediator. Add Hebrews 7:25 and Acts 4:12 to explain Christ's unique saving and priestly work. Do not use 1 Timothy 2:5 to claim that Christians may never pray for one another. Explain that Scripture does not command the invocation of Mary and directs believers to approach God through Christ.
